# Fix: Laptop Stops Working After Liquid Spill on Keyboard

## Context

A liquid spill on a laptop keyboard doesn't always cause immediate failure — the device may keep working for a while before shutting down or behaving erratically. This delay happens because liquid can take time to seep past the keyboard membrane and reach the motherboard, connectors, or battery contacts underneath, where it causes short circuits or corrosion.

Once the laptop stops responding, the priority is to remove power completely and get the liquid out before it causes further (often permanent) damage. Continuing to run the laptop or plugging it back in while still wet is what typically turns a survivable spill into a dead motherboard.

## Symptom

- Laptop was working normally after a spill, then suddenly powered off or froze.
- Keys may stick, double-type, or not respond at all.
- Laptop may fail to power back on, or power on with display/keyboard issues.

## Fix

1. **Power off immediately** and disconnect the charger if it's still plugged in.
2. **Remove the battery** if it's removable. If it's a sealed/internal battery, skip to step 3 and avoid powering the laptop on.
3. **Open the laptop** (remove the back panel/base cover) to access the internals.
4. **Clean the affected area**:
   - Use a dry, lint-free cloth or paper towel to soak up visible liquid.
   - Use isopropyl alcohol (90%+) and a soft brush or cotton swab to clean any sticky residue or spots near connectors, the keyboard ribbon, and the motherboard — sugary or mineral-heavy liquids (juice, soda, coffee) leave residue that causes corrosion even after drying.
5. **Let it dry completely** before reassembling or powering on:
   - Leave it open in a dry, well-ventilated area for at least 24–48 hours.
   - Avoid using rice, hair dryers, or direct heat — these don't dry it faster and can push moisture deeper or cause heat damage.
6. Once fully dry, reassemble the laptop, reconnect the battery, and power it on.

## Notes

- The faster the laptop is powered off after a spill, the better the chance of avoiding permanent damage — never try to "keep using it" once liquid has made contact.
- If the spill was anything other than plain water (coffee, juice, soda), cleaning with isopropyl alcohol is especially important, since sugars and salts left behind continue corroding contacts over time even after everything looks dry.
- If the laptop still doesn't power on after drying, the damage may be at the motherboard level and could require professional repair or component-level diagnosis.
- For laptops with non-removable batteries, disconnecting the battery connector on the motherboard (if accessible) is safer than leaving it connected while cleaning.
